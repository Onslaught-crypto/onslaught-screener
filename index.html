How to read this · settings
  • DMI: ▲ when +DI > −DI and ADX ≥ threshold (trend has strength), ▼ when −DI > +DI and ADX ≥ threshold, · when ADX is below threshold.
  • RSI > EMA: RSI(14) above its own EMA means momentum is improving. RSI > 50: momentum regime.
  • OBV > EMA50: volume flow is confirming price.
  • OI rising: open interest grew by at least the threshold over N candles. New positions are fuelling the move, so it counts toward both the bull and the bear score.
  • MTF: trend direction on 1D, 4H and 1H (3+ of the 4 TA checks). All three agreeing with an aligned setup adds to its rank.
  • Triggers are events in the last N candles (subscript = candles ago): DMI cross (ADX ≥ threshold − 5), RSI crossing 50, RSI crossing its EMA, OBV crossing its EMA, squeeze fired (Bollinger 20/2 leaving Keltner 20/1.5 ATR), Coil (squeeze still on), RVOL (volume ≥ 2× the 20-bar average), and regular or hidden RSI divergences from 3-bar pivots. Green = bullish, red = bearish.
  • ★ Watchlist: star a pair to pin it to the top (Pin ★) or filter to it (★ only, or the Watchlist card). Stars are saved in your browser.
  • Since last scan: pairs that became aligned or triggered (⚡) since your previous scan on the same timeframe, and pairs that lost alignment. New entries get an IN pill and a purple edge in the table. Click a chip to open its trade plan.
  • Telegram: create a bot with @BotFather, paste its token and your chat ID in Settings, press Send test, then tick Send alerts to Telegram. Each alert lists the pairs with triggers, context and (optionally) entry, stop and targets. The token stays in this browser only. The drawer also has a Send to Telegram button for any plan.
  • Alerts: turn on with 🔔 (your browser asks permission). After each scan, new triggered setups (or new alignments too, in Settings) raise a desktop notification, an on-page toast and a sound; the tab title shows a count while you're away. Auto-refresh must be on and the tab must stay open; with alerts on, scans keep running in a background tab.
  • RS 7D: relative strength over the last 7 days in percentage points: ₿ = vs BTC, Ξ = vs ETH (hover for 24h). Green = leading by 2+ points, red = lagging by 2+. Leaders make the best longs in a risk-on market, laggards the best shorts. The regime bar also shows ETH vs BTC: ETH leading often comes before altcoins run. RS is shown, filtered and journaled but not yet part of the grade, so the Journal can show first whether it adds an edge.
  • Grade A / B / C rates the aligned side: +1 score 5/5 · +2 all timeframes agree (+1 if only 1D, −2 if 1D is against) · +1 with the regime (−2 against) · +1 within 1.5 ATR of its average (−2 if stretched 3+) · +1 new positions on its side (−1 for short-covering rallies or long-liquidation drops) · +1 when the other side is crowded (−1 when its own side is) · +1 for 2+ triggers · +1 when a recent confirmed divergence (RSI + MACD, or Strong) supports the side (−1 when one opposes it). A = 5+, B = 2–4, C = below. New listings, extreme funding and thin liquidity cap it at C (shown as !). Hover any grade for its breakdown.
  • LONG / SHORT badge = a triggered setup: 4–5/5 aligned plus a trigger in the same direction. These rank first and have their own stat card.
  • Positioning compares price and OI change over the same N candles: New longs (price ↑ OI ↑), New shorts (price ↓ OI ↑), Short covering (price ↑ OI ↓, a squeeze that often fades once covering ends), Long liquidation (price ↓ OI ↓). Moves under 0.5% count as flat.
  • Funding: the last funding rate. 🔥 marks extremes: at or above the hot threshold longs are crowded, at or below the cold threshold shorts are crowded. Crowded sides are fuel for squeezes against them.
  • Regime bar: BTC trend on 4H and 1D (3+ of the 4 TA checks), breadth across the scanned pairs, and median funding and OI. Aligned setups against the regime get a dashed outline on their score.
  • Ext (ATR): how far the close is from EMA20, measured in ATRs. Above +3 or below −3 (⚠) means the move is stretched. The trend may be real, but entries there have poor risk/reward, so wait for a pullback.
  • Aligned = 4 or 5 of 5 checks in one direction. NEW = alignment appeared on the latest candle (it wasn't aligned on the one before).
  • Hover any check to see the raw values. Click a row to open its trade plan (chart, entry, stop, targets, sizing). The ↗ next to a pair opens TradingView.

How to read the Market Map
  • Across: relative strength over 7 days. Right of centre = the coin beat BTC (or ETH) this week.
  • Up and down: relative strength over the last 24 hours, the recent momentum of that strength.
  • Leading (top right): strong and still gaining. Weakening (bottom right): strong but fading, often taking profit. Lagging (bottom left): weak and still losing. Improving (top left): weak but turning up, where early rotations show first.
  • Coins tend to rotate clockwise: Improving → Leading → Weakening → Lagging. Improving coins with an A or B long setup are early candidates; Weakening coins with a short setup are early fade candidates.
  • Click any dot or name to open its trade plan. Drag a box on the chart to zoom in; double-click or Reset zoom to go back.
  • Spread scale uses a square root so the crowded middle opens up; Linear shows true distances. Values off the chart are pinned to the edge with a dashed outline and their value in the label.
  • A soft glow marks A-grade triggered setups.

How to read this
  • vs Week / vs Month: today's 24h volume divided by the sector's average day over the last 7 / 30 closed days. 1.0 is normal, 2.0 is double. Both high = a real move with fuel. Month high but week flat = it has been hot all week, you may be late. Week high but month flat = something just started.
  • Share Δ: the sector's slice of all volume shown today minus its average slice over the last 7 days, in percentage points. This is the rotation signal.
  • Participation: share of the sector's coins whose own volume is at least 1.5× their weekly normal. High volume with low participation means one coin is pumping, not the sector. This column kills most false alarms.
  • Price (VW): the volume-weighted 24h price change. Volume alone never says who is buying; paired with price, heavy volume and price up = money in, heavy volume and price down = money out.
  • Trend: how many of the sector's coins are bull- or bear-aligned in your last Crypto Scanner scan (scan first to fill it).
  • Coins carry several tags, so a coin's volume counts toward each of its sectors; shares are normalised across the sectors shown. Volume shows where attention is, not where price goes next. Confirm on the chart.

How the macro read works
  • BTC.D: Bitcoin's share of the crypto market. Rising = money concentrating in BTC (or alts bleeding); falling = rotation into alts.
  • USDT.D + USDC.D: share held in the two big stablecoins, the market's cash. Rising = risk-off, money to the sidelines; falling = cash being deployed.
  • TOTAL3ES: value of all alts excluding BTC, ETH and stablecoins. Rising = real money flowing into alts.
  • OTHERS.D: share of everything outside the top 10. Rising = small-cap speculation; a 15%+ jump in 30 days is flagged as froth.
  • Each index gets a direction from its 7-day change plus its 20/50-day average structure. The four directions map to one regime (table above).
  • The regime adds or removes grade points: for example −2 for alt longs in Risk-off, +1 in Alt season. The points appear in each grade's tooltip and in the trade plan.
  • Numbers are rebuilt from CoinGecko (today's market caps and the BTC, ETH, USDT and USDC history) and Binance daily prices (everything else, assuming supply stays constant over the window). They run a few tenths off TradingView's indices because the coin universe differs; the direction is what counts. The TradingView chart shows the official ones.

How to read divergences
  • Bullish divergence: price makes a lower low but the indicator makes a higher low, so selling momentum is fading. Bearish: price makes a higher high but the indicator makes a lower high, so buying momentum is fading. Both are early warnings that a move may turn, not entries on their own.
  • RSI compares the two RSI(14) lows or highs. MACD compares the two swings of the MACD histogram, which must be separate waves (the histogram crosses zero between them). RSI + MACD means both disagree with price on the same two swings, and is the most reliable kind.
  • Swings are found with 5 candles on each side, so a divergence is confirmed five candles after the second swing. Forming ones (shown when "Confirmed only" is off) can disappear with the next candle.
  • Strength (0 to 100) adds up: how many indicators agree, how deep RSI is in oversold or overbought, how far the indicator disagrees, how far price pushed on, confirmation and freshness. 70 or more is Strong.
  • With trend means the divergence points the same way as the EMA20 / EMA50 trend (a pullback or bounce may be ending). Against trend is a reversal attempt: it fails more often, so size down and wait for confirmation.
  • The mini chart shows the last 100 candles. The white line joins the two swings on price and on each indicator. Click a card for the trade plan, already set to the direction of the divergence.
  • The scan covers the top 100 pairs by volume on 15m, 1H, 4H and 1D, using closed candles. Divergences fail often in strong trends. 15m is noisy; 4H and 1D are slower but more reliable.
  • Telegram: with "Divergence alerts" ticked in Settings, you get a message when RSI + MACD both diverge on a confirmed swing, with Strong strength, on 1H, 4H or 1D. The page must stay open and auto-refreshing (the PC runner does the same without a browser).

    How to read the liquidation map
    • It's an estimate. Binance doesn't publish where positions get liquidated. The map assumes that every rise in open interest is new positions opened at that candle's price, split between longs and shorts by who was buying or selling aggressively, and spread across the leverage levels you tick. Each one gets a liquidation price, and the band stays lit until price trades through it.
    • Brighter = more estimated liquidations at that price. Bands above price are short liquidations (forced buying that can fuel a squeeze up); bands below are long liquidations (forced selling that can fuel a flush down).
    • Price is often drawn toward big nearby clusters (liquidity "magnets"), and the move frequently reverses once a cluster has been swept. Use it for timing and for placing stops away from obvious clusters, not as a signal on its own.
    • Falling open interest removes liquidity across the map (positions closed). The right-hand profile shows what is still active now.

    How the journal works
    • After each Crypto Scanner scan with closed candles, it logs every triggered setup (cooldown 6 candles per pair, side and timeframe) and every new alignment (aligned on the latest candle only). It records the context at that moment: score, triggers, regime, MTF, extension, positioning and funding.
    • Outcomes update after each scan, or with Update outcomes: returns after 4, 12 and 24 candles (in your favour = positive), maximum favourable and adverse excursion, and a simulated trade result: Win = +1.5R target hit first, Loss = −1R stop hit first (both in the same candle counts as a loss), Time = neither within 24 candles, closed at market.
    • Avg R is the expectancy per trade in risk units: above 0 means the signal paid over this sample, before fees and slippage.
    • It only logs what you scanned: if the screener is closed, signals in that time are missed. Leave auto-refresh on to build the sample faster. Data stays in this browser; use Backup / Restore to move it.
